The Unanswered Question, Pentadically

Surprise presupposes a shared II. Without it, IV's softmax computes uncertainty over the wrong hypothesis space.
Same cycle, abductive gloss — I Observed → II Expected → III Mismatch → IV Hypotheses → V Trial

Okubala si Kulaba — counting is not seeing.

Bernstein's six 1973 Norton Lectures, run through the pentad in its shared-code reading: I raw substrate, II the code composer and listener must hold in common, III the surprise/threshold event that only means something relative to that shared II, IV a softmax held open across live candidate interpretations, V the falsifiable cadence that closes — or, in two marked cases below, fails to close cleanly — the cycle.

A second name for the same five slots

The wheel below reads two ways at once. I → II → III → IV → V is the musical-perception cycle already in place. Underneath it runs the same skeleton in its bare epistemic form — the shape any disciplined noticing takes, whether the domain is a symphony or a spilled cup of sugar:

Observed (I) is the raw reading, before any frame is applied. Expected (II) is the shared code that reading gets checked against — the diatonic ear, the pheromone trail, the prior. Mismatch (III) is not noise and not confirmation; it's Observed refusing to fit Expected, and it is only legible as mismatch because Expected was held with some confidence to begin with. Hypotheses (IV) is the softmax proper — several live candidate explanations weighted, none yet collapsed. Trial (V) is the falsifiable move that collapses them: a performance, a re-check of the floor, a listened-through cadence.

Framed this way, the two amber breaks in the table below are the same failure stated twice: Tristan (4/III) skips straight to Mismatch without saying what Expected it's measured against; Mahler 9 (5/V) asserts a Trial-result with no Hypotheses stage shown first. Both are the cycle entered mid-loop.

Fig. 1 — the pentad as a closed cycle, doubly labeled: teal for the musical-perception reading, amber for the underlying Observed → Expected → Mismatch → Hypotheses → Trial skeleton. III's surprise/mismatch is only well-defined relative to the II/Expected two parties already share; break that link and IV's softmax/hypotheses inherits a miscalibrated support set.

Worked example — household ant colony

You spill sugar. Ants find it. Over hours, foot traffic reinforces the pheromone trail — II — until it's a strong, two-way highway back to the mess. You wipe the sugar away.

The colony keeps running the highway. That is the surprise-defining condition: strong II (a well-reinforced, high-confidence code) meeting a I that no longer matches what II predicts. No sugar, but the trail still says sugar. That mismatch — not the trail's mere existence, not the ants' mere presence — is III.

This is the clean formal case Bernstein never quite states: surprise isn't strong signal, and it isn't weak signal. It's a confident signal contradicted by I. A faint, uncertain trail meeting an empty floor isn't surprising — the ants were barely committed to begin with. It's precisely the strength of II that makes the mismatch at III legible as surprise rather than noise. The same asymmetry is what's missing every time Bernstein calls a passage "ambiguous" without specifying how strongly held the listener's prior actually was.

In the abductive gloss: Observed = empty floor. Expected = strong trail-code predicting sugar. Mismatch = trail contradicted by floor. Hypotheses = scout ants fanning out to alternative sources, trail strength held provisionally across several candidates. Trial = which alternate trail gets reinforced by the next round of foot traffic — the empirical vote that collapses the softmax.

Amber cells break the cycle rather than fill it. Tristan (4/III) declares surprise without naming whose II it's relative to. Mahler 9 (5/V) asserts a cadence with no III or IV shown first. Stravinsky's polytonality (6/IV) isn't miscalibrated softmax — two keys running at once means two support sets superimposed, which the five-position cycle doesn't currently have a slot for. In the abductive gloss, that last case reads as two live Expected-codes generating hypotheses in parallel — a genuine open problem for the model, not a filled cell.

The Six Lectures

1 · Musical PhonologyOct 9, 1973

Watch for the moment I (the overtone series) quietly becomes II (a specific diatonic code) — the Balinese pentatonic aside is where a foreign II gets treated as if it reduces to the same physical substrate.

2 · Musical SyntaxOct 1973

The Mozart 40 rewrite is the cleanest empirical test in the whole series: is "nightmare of symmetry" a property of the music, or of the 18th-century periodic-phrase II Bernstein's ear already holds?

3 · Musical SemanticsOct 23, 1973

Watch whether Bernstein can actually hold the Pastoral as pure intrinsic transformation, or whether the extrinsic shepherd-and-storm content keeps leaking back into his own commentary — you can't cleanly subtract a code you're already inside of.

4 · The Delights and Dangers of Ambiguity1973

The whole lecture is the shared-II test at scale. When Tristan is called "maximally ambiguous," ask: ambiguous relative to which fixed support set — Wagner's contemporaries, or a 1973 Harvard ear with a century more chromatic exposure?

5 · The Twentieth Century Crisis1973

Two different IIs are required to both be true at once here — twelve-tone "fighting" tonal hearing, while also being judged rigorous on its own terms. Mark exactly where the Mahler 9 Adagio commentary drops analysis for pure assertion.

6 · The Poetry of Earth1973

Stravinsky's polytonality is the one passage that doesn't fit the cycle at all — worth timestamping as the place the pentad's own five-position model needs pressure, not another correction inside it.
